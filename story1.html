Story 1 · CPF

Three neighbours. Same birthday. Very different retirements.

This is an illustrative story with made-up people. The retirement sums and payout estimates are CPF Board's official 2026 figures. Last checked: 9 October 2026.

Mdm Lim, Mr Tan and Mr Ong live on the same floor and all three turn 55 in 2026. Same age, the same salary of SGD$5,000 a month, similar lives. The difference is a habit.

Years ago, Mdm Lim started opening her CPF statement once a year. She learned what each account was for, kept her MediSave healthy, and topped up her own CPF when she could. Mr Tan said, "CPF will sort itself out," and never looked. Mr Ong went one step further than Mdm Lim: he prepared early. For years he set money aside on purpose, so that at 55 he could top up his Retirement Account to the Enhanced Retirement Sum without a scramble.

On their 55th birthday, CPF sets up a Retirement Account (RA) for each of them, and their Special Account (SA) closes. What counts now is how much sits in the RA, because that money buys a monthly income for life through CPF LIFE (Lifelong Income For the Elderly).

Payouts are CPF Board's estimates for members turning 55 in 2026 (CPF article, 2 September 2026). CPF calculates them for a male member on the CPF LIFE Standard Plan, so a woman or a member on another plan will see different amounts. Your own figure depends on your sex, plan, age and what you set aside. Use CPF's monthly payout estimator for your own number.

Compare the three, every month for life (estimates)

  • Mdm Lim gets about SGD$830 more than Mr Tan, roughly SGD$9,960 a year.
  • Mr Ong gets about SGD$1,660 more than Mdm Lim, roughly SGD$19,920 a year.
  • Mr Ong gets about SGD$2,490 more than Mr Tan, roughly SGD$29,880 a year, which is about 3.6 times Mr Tan's monthly income.

The extra is not luck. It is how much each of them chose to set aside at 55. The rest of this guide shows how they did it.

The trade-off for Mr Ong: to reach the Enhanced Retirement Sum he had to set aside SGD$220,400 more than Mdm Lim did, from his CPF savings or his own cash. That money is locked in for retirement payouts and cannot be taken back, so it only makes sense if he will not need it for other things. Whether it is right depends on your own situation.

The quiet hero: compounding interest

Between 55 and 65, none of the three adds a cent. Their Retirement Accounts simply sit and earn the guaranteed 4% a year. Each year's interest is added to the balance, so next year they earn interest on the interest too. That is compounding, and it is why the gap keeps widening.

Of which, interest on interestSGD$8,843SGD$17,686SGD$35,372
  • Mr Ong's interest alone is about SGD$211,700. That is nearly double the entire amount Mr Tan set aside (SGD$110,200), earned just by waiting.
  • Interest on interest is real money. Plain (non-compounding) interest of 4% for 10 years would give Mr Ong SGD$176,320. Compounding gives him SGD$35,372 more, for doing nothing extra.
  • Double the sum, double the interest. Each person sets aside twice what the one before did, so each earns twice as much interest.
  • Time is the other ingredient. The earlier money goes in, the more years it has to compound. That is why Ethan's story below starts at 22, not 55.

How we worked this out: balance at 65 = sum set aside at 55 × 1.0410, and 1.0410 = 1.480244 (multiplying 1.04 by itself 10 times), with no withdrawals and nothing added. 4% a year is the lowest rate CPF currently guarantees on the Retirement Account and is reviewed every three months, so real interest could be higher. The extra interest on the first SGD$60,000 of combined balances (2% on the first SGD$30,000 and 1% on the next SGD$30,000 for members 55 and above) is left out, so the true figures could be higher still. CPF's own illustration shows slightly different balances at 65 (SGD$170,200, SGD$330,100 and SGD$650,100) because it uses its own interest assumptions. This is a simple illustration, not a forecast of your own account.

Prepare early: the difference between a scramble and a plan

Mr Ong needed SGD$220,400 more than Mdm Lim at 55 to reach the Enhanced Retirement Sum. Finding that sum in one go at 55 is hard. Building it slowly, from early on, is far easier, and compounding does a large share of the work.

  • Start at 55: your money has about 10 years to compound before payouts. In the examples above, interest is about a third of the final balance (32%).
  • Start at 22: your first dollars can compound for up to 43 years, 33 years before 55 and 10 more before 65. In Ethan's example, interest is about 69% of his balance at 65, more than two-thirds (Ethan's story shows how we worked this out).
  • A plan has costs too. In Ethan's example he adds SGD$64,000 of his own cash in his first eight working years. But the bigger lever is one move at 55, which needs no cash at all. Ethan's story shows how.
  • Every year you wait has a cost, because that year's compounding cannot be won back later.

Whatever your age, the best time to start is now. Open your CPF statement this week, then read Ethan's story below.
